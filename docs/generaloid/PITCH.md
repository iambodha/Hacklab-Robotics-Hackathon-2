# Generaloid: robots are connectors, intelligence is a client

A robot application should survive a change of arm or AI model. Generaloid makes both replaceable while keeping **NormaCore Station at the centre**: the single owner of motor buses, cameras and commands. Recording captures what the robot actually saw and did. MCP gives any compatible client the tools to act.

The practical result is one platform with robot connectors. ElRobot brings the calibrated seven-axis arm, gripper, cameras, safety limits and twin that worked at this hackathon. SO-101 brings a five-axis model, its own joint/gripper mapping, IK and twin. A third robot supplies those declarations and a mount adapter rather than adding robot-name branches throughout the server. Profiles keep calibration numbers out of the orchestration code.

The model is a client choice. Claude, Gemini, Devin or another MCP client can list tools and use the same robot surface. A Gemini advisor can propose actions; a human can review them through the dashboard. Neither is required for the MCP server to run. The executor supplies a single motion owner, while learning stores episodes, versioned skill parameters and proposals rather than hiding improvement inside a conversation.

The bottle cap is the evidence that this is more than an interface diagram. The shared project record for 22 November reports that ElRobot initially only loosened a cap, then opened it in attempts 9 and 10 after a top-down, least-lean approach. Later demonstration takes opened a fully closed cap in 10–11 strokes followed by a 6 cm lift (`logs/learning/bottle/SUMMARY.md` in the hackathon records). Generaloid wraps those working tools without rewriting their mechanics. The twin MCP check exercises the same episode and `twist_cap` flow against a simulated screw cap and independently checks whether the threads release. A simulated release is distinct from the earlier physical demonstration and from lifting the cap clear.

The surrounding digital-twin work also lowers hardware requirements. Ordinary cameras plus ArUco markers provide metric alignment; the Gaussian-splat pipeline reconstructs appearance without a depth sensor. MuJoCo supplies contact and motion physics. Recorder and dashboard make attempts inspectable, and the learning loop turns outcomes into explicit changes that another model or operator can review.

This release is deliberately additive. ElRobot's existing entry points remain intact, and Generaloid's tool-parity tests preserve its interface. SO-101 has offline IK, twin motion and fake-Station coverage; its real calibration is still unverified and its current twin and real frames differ. The direction is concrete: qualify another connector, preserve a stable Station boundary, and carry the same skills, evidence and client workflow across hardware.
